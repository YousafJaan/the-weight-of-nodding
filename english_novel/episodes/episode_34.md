# Episode 34: The Slap of Rejection
### The Sugar Mill Dynasty, The Car Demand, and The Sovereign Eviction

**Season 4 : The Crucible of Intellect** | Words: 920

> **Synopsis:** As Rija's academic fame spreads, a wealthy feudal sugar mill family visits with an arrogant marriage proposal demanding massive dowry; Rija personally confronts and evicts them from the ancestral home.

---

Human greed possesses a grotesque, shape-shifting genius. As my analytical newspaper columns gained widespread acclaim and word reached our rural tehsil that my MPhil thesis was being championed by national jurists, the very society that had declared me 'ruined' and 'unmarriageable' suddenly executed a breathtaking, hypocritical pivot.
 
The most dramatic manifestation of this hypocrisy occurred on a crisp November Sunday.
 
A convoy of three gleaming, black four-wheel-drive luxury SUVs rolled into our dusty village lane, kicking up plumes of yellow dust, coming to a halt directly outside our modest wooden gate.
 
It was Chaudhry Asghar : a multi-millionaire feudal landlord, provincial political powerbroker, and owner of two major sugar mills in the district : accompanied by his wife, his eldest son Kamran, and a retinue of armed private guards carrying Kalashnikovs.
 
Kamran was a thirty-year-old foreign-educated executive who wore designer Italian suits and gold Rolex watches, yet carried the unmistakable, lazy arrogance of inherited feudal wealth.
 
The entire village was paralyzed with shock. Taya Hakim Khan rushed out from his compound in his finest silk turban, falling over himself to bow before Chaudhry Asghar, escorting the wealthy delegation into our family baithak with obsequious sycophancy.
 
Father was completely overwhelmed, his hands trembling as he served tea in our best vintage glass cups.
 
"Sajjad Sahib," Chaudhry Asghar announced, puffing on an imported Havana cigar and tapping his gold-headed cane against the floor. "We have observed your daughter's rising public profile. My son Kamran requires an articulate, educated wife who can represent our family at embassy functions and high-society events in Islamabad. We are willing to overlook her humble rural origins and the past hostel controversies."
 
Father smiled nervously, looking as though he had won the lottery.
 
"However," Chaudhry Asghar continued, his tone turning transactional and razor-sharp, "in our social circle, a bridal procession does not leave our estate without appropriate customary honors. We expect a brand-new Honda Civic sedan registered in Kamran's name, ten tolas of pure gold sets, and the transfer of your five acres of canal land to demonstrate your family's commitment. A girl who has lived independently in the city must bring significant collateral to guarantee her compliance in our household."
 
Father's smile instantly froze. His face turned pale, his head sinking between his shoulders in crushing despair. He knew that selling every brick of our house would not raise a fraction of that ransom.

The heavy wooden door of the baithak swung open.
 
I stepped into the room.
 
I did not carry a tea tray, nor was my head bowed in traditional maidenly shame. In my hands, I carried a bound copy of the Punjab Protection of Women Against Violence Act and my fountain pen.
 
"Rija! Leave this room immediately!" Taya Hakim Khan barked, his face dark with panic that I would spoil the lucrative connection.
 
I completely ignored the old patriarch, stepping directly before Chaudhry Asghar and his son.
 
"Chaudhry Sahib," I said, my voice cold, steady, and carrying the authority of an empress. "You did not travel to this village seeking a wife for your son. You traveled here like a vulgar, greedy blackmailer seeking to plunder an honest man's home under the guise of marriage."
 
Chaudhry Asghar's cigar dropped onto his silk trousers. Kamran jumped up, his face flushing crimson with fury. "How dare you speak to my father like that, you insolent village girl?"
 
"I dare speak the truth because your millions cannot purchase my silence!" I replied, stepping toward him, my eyes blazing with righteous disgust. "Your son has a foreign master's degree, yet his soul is as bankrupt as a roadside beggar! You possess sugar mills, luxury vehicles, and vast estates, yet you come to a poor farmer's courtyard demanding cars and land as a bribe to accept his daughter! Have you no shame? Have you no fear of God?"
 
"Shut your mouth!" Kamran snarled, taking an aggressive step toward me.
 
"Do not take another step!" I commanded, raising my hand with such fierce, absolute authority that the young millionaire froze in his tracks. "Listen to me, Chaudhry Kamran : my intellect, my character, and my honor are sovereign treasures that your family's entire empire could never afford to purchase! I reject your proposal with the contempt it deserves! Now take your cigars, your armed guards, and your insatiable greed, and get out of our home before I summon the district magistrate!"
 
Chaudhry Asghar rose, shaking with apoplectic humiliation, knocking over his tea cup. "You are insane!" he sputtered, marching toward the door. "Sajjad, your daughter is an untamable, demonic creature! She will die alone in this mud!"
 
They stormed out of the compound, slamming their SUV doors and speeding away in a cloud of dust.
 
Taya Hakim Khan clutched his chest, wailing in despair : "We are ruined! She has insulted the most powerful landlord of the district!"
 
Then, a miracle occurred.
 
My father slowly rose from his charpai. He looked at Taya Hakim Khan, then turned his gaze toward me. For the first time in my life, there was no fear, no doubt, and no shame in his eyes.
 
"Brother Hakim," Father said, his voice quiet yet carrying an unfamiliar, magnificent dignity. "My daughter was right. We may be poor farmers, but we are not beggars. I would rather see Rija remain in this courtyard with her books for the rest of her days than sell her soul to arrogant wolves."
 
A wave of warmth flooded my heart. My father had finally found his backbone. The victory was complete.
