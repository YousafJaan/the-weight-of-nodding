# Episode 20: The Fist in Mid-Air
### The Ambush of the Nikahnama, The Brother's Strike, and The Mother's Shield

**Season 2 : The Courtyard of Chains** | Words: 956

> **Synopsis:** The climactic showdown of Season 2 : Aunt Shabana ambushes the home with a compromised cleric for a forced marriage, brother Hamza raises his fist to strike Rija, and Mother performs an unprecedented act of defiance, catching the blow in mid-air.

---

The ambush was executed on a sweltering Thursday afternoon, carefully timed to coincide with Father's absence at the grain market in the distant tehsil headquarters.
 
At two o'clock, the courtyard gate was pushed open with unceremonious force. Aunt Shabana strode in, flanked by her husband Uncle Farooq, a nervous, sweating village nikah registrar clutching a bound leather ledger, and her delinquent son Arslan, who was dressed in an ill-fitting silk kurta, reeking of cheap musk and pan.
 
Behind them marched Hamza, his face flushed with triumph.
 
"Bhabhi!" Aunt Shabana announced with imperial authority, confronting Mother in the dawan. "The time for foolish delays is over. Sajjad gave us his verbal authorization before he boarded the bus this morning. He said, 'Settle this matter before I return so the shame is washed away.' The cleric is here. Witnesses are present. Call Rija to place her thumbprint on the ledger, and we will take her to our compound before the afternoon prayer."
 
Mother's face went chalk-white. "Shabana! Sajjad told me no such thing! He promised he would never solemnize this union without speaking to me!"
 
"Your husband is a coward who cannot face his own daughter!" Uncle Farooq interrupted aggressively. "We are the elders of this clan. The nikah will be solemnized right now!"
 
I stepped out from the inner doorway, holding my chin high, my black chador fastened securely around my chest.
 
"Not a single person will sign anything in this house," I said, my voice cutting through their noise like an icy blade. "This is an illegal assembly. If this cleric opens that ledger inside this private home without my father's physical presence and without my free consent, I will file a criminal petition under the Protection of Women Act against every single individual standing in this courtyard."
 
Arslan sneered, stepping forward, his red, betel-stained teeth bared in an ugly grin. "Listen to the high-court advocate! You think the police will listen to a dishonored girl over Taya Hakim Khan? Once this ink is on the paper, my sweet cousin, I will teach you what real law is."
 
"Stand back, you degenerate!" I warned, pointing toward the gate. "Do not dare to take another step toward me!"
 
Hamza exploded with uncontrollable, berserk rage.
 
"You filthy, rebellious dog!" he roared, lunging across the courtyard, his right fist clenching into an iron hammer as he drew back his arm to deliver a vicious, bone-shattering blow directly toward my face!

The blow would have fractured my jaw and knocked me unconscious.
 
I did not close my eyes. I did not flinch.
 
Yet, before that violent fist could travel six inches toward my skin, an astonishing, unprecedented phenomenon occurred.
 
A hand : thin, wrinkled, scarred from decades of burns over clay hearths and cracked with hard labor : shot out with the blinding velocity of a striking cobra.
 
It was Mother's hand.
 
Her small, calloused fingers wrapped with superhuman, motherly fury around Hamza's thick wrist, arresting his massive fist completely in mid-air!
 
The entire courtyard froze into petrified, disbelieving shock.
 
Mother, the submissive, silent, terrified matriarch who had spent twenty-five years bowing her head to every insult, who had cowered in the corners while the men shouted, who had swallowed her own tears in the dark : stood before her six-foot-tall armed son, transformed into a roaring, primeval lioness defending her cub.
 
Her eyes blazed with a terrifying, apocalyptic jallal that I had never witnessed in any human being.
 
"Strike her, Hamza!" Mother's voice was not a scream; it was a deep, guttural roar that shook the very foundations of the mud walls. "Strike your sister! But know this : the instant your hand touches her flesh, I will tear my own clothes, walk barefoot into the center of the village square, and proclaim before the entire world that Sajjad's son is a murderer and a beast! I will smash my skull against the mosque minaret, and my blood will curse you and your children to the seventh generation!"
 
She stepped forward, forcing Hamza's arm backward with sheer, incandescent moral fury until the young man : armed with his pistol and his tribal arrogance : took an involuntary, stumbling step backward, his face draining of all color.
 
Mother turned her blazing gaze upon Aunt Shabana, Farooq, and the trembling cleric.
 
"Get out of my courtyard!" she snarled, pointing her shaking finger at the open gate. "Take your delinquent son and your fraudulent ledger and get out before I pour boiling oil over your heads! There will be no nikah here! There will be no surrender here! Get out!"
 
Uncle Farooq muttered a curse, grabbed the terrified cleric by his collar, and shoved him toward the street. Aunt Shabana, trembling with genuine shock and fury, pulled Arslan backward through the gate, screaming hysterical threats of tribal vengeance as they retreated into the alley.
 
Hamza stared at Mother for three long seconds, his chest heaving, his eyes wide with disbelieving shock. Without a single word, he turned, kicked the courtyard door open, and fled into the lane.
 
The gate slammed shut.
 
The moment the courtyard fell silent, Mother's knees gave way, and she collapsed onto the dirt floor. I dropped beside her, wrapping my arms around her trembling body, burying my face into her lap as both of us wept uncontrollably : tears of release, tears of shock, and tears of profound, historic victory.
 
Season Two had drawn to its magnificent, earth-shaking conclusion. The chains of fear had not merely been loosened; they had been shattered by the supreme, invincible power of a mother's awakened love.
 
The dark night of oppression was ending. From the ashes of the courtyard, the dawn of self-reliance, higher education, and absolute triumph was preparing to rise.
