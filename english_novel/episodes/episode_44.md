# Episode 44: The Speech to the Nation
### The Live Broadcast, The Message of Surah An-Nur, and The Daughter's Manifesto

**Season 5 : The Turban and the Dawn** | Words: 558

> **Synopsis:** Addressing the national assembly and millions watching live on television, Rija delivers a historic, electrifying manifesto on women's rights, dedicating her victory to the suffering mothers of Pakistan.

---

When I placed my hands upon the mahogany edges of the presidential lectern and looked into the cameras broadcasting live to millions of homes across Pakistan, all nervousness evaporated from my consciousness.
 
I was no longer an isolated student; I was an instrument of historical reckoning.
 
"Bismillah-ir-Rahman-ir-Rahim," my voice resonated through the state auditorium with crystal, silver clarity.
 
"Mr. President, distinguished jurists, and my fellow citizens across Pakistan : I accept this national honor not as a personal triumph, but as a solemn deposit on behalf of those whose voices have been systematically choked behind the mud walls of our rural hinterlands.
 
"For centuries, our society has operated under a monstrous, hypocritical double standard. When a son is born into an agrarian household, drums are beaten, feasts are prepared, and he is celebrated as the inheritor of the soil. But when a daughter opens her eyes, the room falls silent, and she is treated as a temporary guest, a potential liability, a fragile glass cup waiting to shatter.
 
"We are told by feudal patriarchs that our culture is based on female honor. But let me ask this nation today : what kind of honor is it that requires the economic starvation of its own daughters? What kind of honor is it that coerces young girls to renounce their Quranic inheritance at gunpoint? What kind of honor is it that threatens an educated sister with death because she desires to stand upon her own feet?"
 
A profound, pin-drop silence settled over the vast assembly. Cabinet ministers and high court judges leaned forward, captivated by the raw moral power of the address.

I raised my right hand, pointing toward the television cameras.
 
"To the fathers of Pakistan, I say this : look upon your daughters not with terror of what the world will say, but with the reverence that your Creator commanded! A daughter is not a curse upon your turban; she is the shield that will protect you from the fire of hell! Give her books, give her your trust, and she will elevate your name before the kings of the earth!
 
"To the daughters of Pakistan, who sit in windowless rooms, who weep over unfulfilled dreams, who are told that their destiny is only submission to abuse : hear my voice today!
 
"You are not weak! You are not disqualified! You are the daughters of Hazrat Khadijah and Hazrat Aisha! Never bow your heads before domestic tyranny! Hold fast to the truth, arm yourselves with education, and when every earthly door is bolted against you, remember the promise of Surah An-Nur : that truth cannot be extinguished by the whispers of the wicked!
 
"I dedicate this Gold Medal to my mother : whose calloused hands caught the violent blow meant for my face, whose bridal silver melted so that my mind could flourish, and whose silent prayers opened the gates of this presidential palace.
 
"Pakistan Zindabad!"
 
The moment I finished, the entire auditorium erupted into an explosive, thunderous ovation.
 
Supreme Court judges wiped tears from their eyes; foreign ambassadors nodded in solemn admiration; and in the front row, Father was weeping openly, his chest heaving with uncontrollable emotion, while Mother held her prayer beads against her lips.
 
My voice had crossed the mountains, traveling into millions of homes, igniting a flame of hope that would never be extinguished.
