# Episode 18: Funeral of Blood Ties
### The Shattered Idol, The Mercenary Clan, and The Sovereign Spirit

**Season 2 : The Courtyard of Chains** | Words: 625

> **Synopsis:** Listening through the storeroom crack as family elders negotiate her forced marriage as a real estate transaction, Rija conducts an internal funeral for patriarchal kinship, achieving total psychological independence.

---

On the tenth evening of my imprisonment, an unexpected council convened directly outside the storeroom wall in the rear courtyard. Because the mud plaster had eroded near the foundation, every word spoken by the elders drifted into my dark chamber with startling acoustic clarity.
 
Taya Hakim Khan had arrived with his eldest son, Farooq, and my father. They had brought glasses of tea and were sitting upon rope charpais within six feet of my locked door.
 
"Listen carefully, Sajjad," Taya's gravelly voice announced, accompanied by the familiar bubble of his hookah. "The girl has been locked inside for ten days. Her pride is sufficiently broken. We have spoken with the sub-registrar in the tehsil office. We will execute two documents on Friday : first, her nikahnama with Arslan; second, a general power of attorney signed by her, transferring her future inheritance claim upon your five acres of canal land directly to Arslan as part of the family settlement."
 
My father's voice, hesitant and broken, replied : "Brother... the land is meant for Hamza and the girls' security..."
 
"Hamza has already consented!" Farooq chimed in with a sharp, greedy laugh. "We have promised Hamza a fifty percent share in our tubewell tractor. Sajjad Uncle, look at the reality : your daughter is damaged goods! If we do not bind her to Arslan, she will run away to the courts, demand her legal share under statutory law, and drag our entire family through public litigation. By having her sign the transfer before the marriage, we settle the property dispute forever."
 
Sitting in the dark upon the dirt floor, listening to these men casually barter away my life, my body, and my legal rights like an unwanted strip of dry agricultural wasteland, a terrifying, crystalline calm settled over my soul.
 
All my life, I had been conditioned to revere these men. I had been taught that an uncle was a second father, that a brother was a lifelong guardian, that family honor was a sacred, celestial entity demanding absolute female sacrifice.
 
In that single hour, the false idol of patriarchal kinship was completely, permanently smashed to atoms.

These men were not guardians; they were not protectors; they were not honorable patriarchs. They were petty, terrified feudal mercenaries who used the sacred rhetoric of family honor to disguise their insatiable greed for five acres of mud and canal water!
 
Right there, in the pitch-black silence of the grain storeroom, with dust in my mouth and fever in my veins, I conducted an internal funeral service for my entire extended family.
 
I buried the father who chose village cowardice over his daughter's innocence. I buried the brother who pressed a shotgun to his sister's temple for the sake of a tractor share. I buried the uncles, the aunts, the cousins, and the entire ancestral clan whose love was contingent upon my absolute, unconditional enslavement.
 
They were dead to me. Every single one of them.
 
I raised my hands in the dark, touching the rough mud wall, and I whispered into the silence : *'O Lord of the downtrodden, bear witness : I have divorced this false family. I have severed the chains of their blood. From this moment onward, I belong only to Truth, to Justice, and to You.'*
 
When a person mourns their family while they are still alive, they become invincible. The worst thing a family can do to an individual is to threaten them with abandonment and excommunication. But when the individual has already conducted their funeral in advance, that threat becomes completely powerless.
 
I lay down upon the burlap sacks, closed my eyes, and for the first time in ten days, I slept with the deep, unshakeable tranquility of an empress who has claimed her throne.
