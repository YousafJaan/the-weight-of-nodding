# Episode 33: The Academic Inquisitors
### The Preliminary Scrutiny, The Hostile Committee, and The Wall of Facts

**Season 4 : The Crucible of Intellect** | Words: 744

> **Synopsis:** At the formal pre-defense departmental scrutiny, conservative faculty members launch an aggressive attempt to disqualify Rija's research, only to be crushed under the weight of her empirical documentation.

---

Six weeks prior to the final public defense, every MPhil thesis was required to pass through the departmental Pre-Submission Scrutiny Committee : a closed-door tribunal of five senior professors tasked with evaluating methodology, legal citations, and academic integrity.
 
For me, that committee room was not a forum of scholarly inquiry; it was an ambush engineered by the remnants of the university's conservative establishment.
 
Leading the panel was Dr. Arshad, flanked by two senior traditionalist professors who had long maintained close personal and financial ties with prominent feudal families in the Multan division.
 
When I entered the room and placed five bound copies of my 350-page thesis upon the mahogany table, the atmosphere was thick with palpable hostility. Dr. Arshad did not even open the front cover; he tapped his gold pen impatiently against the table.
 
"Miss Rija," Dr. Arshad began, his voice dripping with condescending skepticism. "The committee has reviewed your chapter outlines. We find your conclusions regarding systemic revenue fraud to be sensationalist, inflammatory, and legally unsupportable. You claim that over ninety percent of rural women in three tehsils are coerced into land renunciation. That is a grave, scandalous allegation against the provincial revenue administration and our ancestral cultural institutions. Unless you tone down these accusations and remove the specific district case studies, this committee cannot approve your submission for external evaluation."
 
The intent was clear : they wanted to castrate my research, stripping away the names, the dates, the court records, and the empirical evidence, reducing my thesis to a toothless, generic theoretical essay that could threaten no one.
 
I sat straight in my chair, looking directly at Dr. Arshad without a flicker of fear.
 
"Dr. Arshad," I said, opening my master briefcase and placing an eight-inch-thick supplementary binder onto the table with an authoritative thud. "You state that my findings are unsupportable. Here are the certified copies of ninety-four registered land mutations obtained under the Freedom of Information Act from the Deputy Commissioner's office. Here are the verified signatures of forty-two revenue patwaris. And here are the stamped judicial affidavits of thirty-six female petitioners currently pending before the Multan Bench of the Lahore High Court."
 
The professors stared at the colossal stack of official legal documentation, their eyes widening in sudden, trapped disbelief.

I flipped open the binder to a flagged page, pointing directly to a certified property mutation from Muzaffargarh.
 
"Consider Case Study 14 on page 112," I continued, my voice echoing with razor-sharp legal precision. "The mutation record states that a nineteen-year-old daughter named Kulsoom Bibi 'voluntarily renounced' her share of forty acres of canal-irrigated wheat land in favor of her paternal uncle on November 12, 2012. Yet, here is the official death certificate issued by the Union Council, proving that Kulsoom Bibi had passed away of tuberculosis three weeks prior to the alleged date of her voluntary appearance before the registrar!"
 
Dr. Arshad's face turned an ugly, mottled shade of crimson. He opened his mouth, but no sound emerged.
 
"This is not sensationalism, Professor," I said, leaning forward, my voice low, intense, and unyielding. "This is criminal forgery under Section 468 of the Pakistan Penal Code. If this committee rejects this thesis on grounds of factual inadequacy, I will immediately submit this entire evidentiary dossier, along with a formal complaint of academic censorship, to the Higher Education Commission's Quality Assurance Cell in Islamabad and the Supreme Court Human Rights Cell."
 
The other two committee members hurriedly exchanged nervous, alarmed glances. They knew that if an investigative inquiry were opened by the HEC, their own compromised supervisory records would be scrutinized.
 
Dr. Masood, who had been sitting quietly at the back of the room observing the proceedings with a quiet, knowing smile, finally cleared his throat and stepped forward.
 
"Gentlemen," Dr. Masood said, placing a firm hand upon the table. "Miss Rija has compiled an unassailable empirical masterpiece. The evidence is certified, the methodology is flawless, and the constitutional analysis is peerless. Are there any further procedural objections?"
 
The room remained utterly silent.
 
Dr. Arshad swallowed hard, picked up his pen with a trembling hand, and scrawled his signature across the clearance certificate.
 
"Approved for external defense," he muttered, avoiding my eyes.
 
I gathered my binders, bowed courteously, and walked out of the faculty wing.
 
I had faced the academic inquisitors in their private chamber and forced them to sign their own retreat. The final summit : the public defense in the Senate Hall : was now within sight.
