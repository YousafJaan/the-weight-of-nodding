# Episode 14: The Double-Barrel Shotgun on the Stairs
### The Dark Landing, The Cold Steel at the Temple, and The Unbroken Will

**Season 2 : The Courtyard of Chains** | Words: 757

> **Synopsis:** Cornered on the dark staircase by her brother Hamza wielding a double-barrel shotgun, Rija faces down mortal terror, choosing the threat of death over the surrender of her conscience.

---

The confrontation occurred on the third afternoon following the disastrous panchayat. The household was engulfed in a state of undeclared civil war : Father had not spoken a single syllable to Mother, while Aunt Shabana visited twice a day to orchestrate the social boycott that had begun to isolate our family from the village water wells.
 
It was three in the afternoon, the hour when the rural plains succumb to a heavy, sweltering lethargy. Mother had finally fallen into an exhausted sleep inside the inner bedroom, and Father was away at the flour mill.
 
I had climbed the narrow, outdoor brick staircase leading to the flat mud roof to bring down the laundry before the evening dust storm arrived. As I turned on the dark, enclosed landing between the two flights of steps, a heavy shadow detached itself from the wall.
 
Hamza was standing there.
 
He was stripped to his white cotton undershirt, his eyes bloodshot, smelling heavily of country liquor and sweat. In his hands, he held Father's old English twelve-gauge double-barrel shotgun. Both hammers were cocked back, their menacing metallic clicks echoing off the brick walls.
 
Before I could take a step backward, his left hand seized my throat with crushing force, slamming my spine against the rough kiln-fired bricks.
 
"You think you won that debate in the baithak, don't you?" he whispered, his voice trembling with manic, uncontrolled fury. "You humiliated Taya Hakim Khan in front of the entire clan! You made my father look like an eunuch who cannot control his own daughter!"
 
The twin steel barrels of the shotgun rose, pressing with brutal force against my right temple. The coldness of the oiled metal was shocking against my burning skin.
 
"Listen to me, Rija," he growled, his finger tightening visibly around the front trigger. "Out here, law is what comes out of these two barrels. I will give you sixty seconds to walk down these stairs, fall at Father's feet, and agree to marry Arslan. If you refuse, I will blow your head into pieces right on this landing. I will tell the village elders that an intruder climbed the roof to violate our honor, and that I eliminated the shame. The police inspector is Taya's first cousin; no one will ask a single question."
 
My lungs fought for oxygen beneath his iron grip. The sharp edge of a brick was digging painfully into my shoulder blade. In that terrifying suspension of time, the entire world narrowed to the circular steel rim pressing against the bone of my skull.
 
Every evolutionary instinct of self-preservation screamed at me to yield : to nod, to weep, to beg for mercy, to promise anything simply to push that cold steel away from my brain.
 
Yet, deep within the deepest foundations of my spirit, a strange, absolute calm descended. It was that profound, mystical clarity that arrives when an individual realizes that the physical death of the body is vastly preferable to the permanent, living death of the soul.
 
I looked directly into my brother's bloodshot, manic eyes, and my voice did not shake.
 
"Pull the trigger, Hamza," I whispered, every word measured and deliberate. "Do it. Pull the trigger. But know this : your bullet can extinguish my breath, but it can never force my hand to sign away my dignity. If you kill me, you will spend the rest of your miserable life running from the shadow of your own cowardice. A man who murders an unarmed sister to protect his fragile ego is not a warrior; he is a pathetic beast. Pull the trigger."
 
For five agonizing seconds, his finger trembled upon the curved steel. I saw the wild, terrifying confusion flare in his eyes. He had anticipated terror, tears, and pathetic groveling. He had no vocabulary for a sister who looked down the barrels of his gun and welcomed death over submission.
 
With a guttural curse, he viciously shoved the butt of the weapon into my ribs and stepped back.
 
"You are insane!" he spit, lowering the hammers with shaking thumbs. "You are completely possessed by the devil! But you will not escape this house. If I do not kill you with lead, this family will kill you with starvation!"
 
He turned and stormed down the staircase. I slid down the rough brick wall, collapsing onto the dusty steps, clutching my bruised ribs as the tears finally came : not tears of defeat, but the burning tears of a soul that had stared into the abyss of death and refused to bow.
