# Episode 30: Breaking the Silence
### The University Auditorium, The Feudal Inquisitors, and The Clarion Call

**Season 3 : The Mantle of Light** | Words: 887

> **Synopsis:** Presenting preliminary field findings at an international academic symposium, Rija shatters the polite hypocrisy of conservative scholars, using Islamic jurisprudence and empirical data to dismantle feudal patriarchy.

---

The decisive public breakthrough of my research career arrived in the spring of my final MPhil year, when Bahauddin Zakariya University hosted the Biennial Inter-University Symposium on Constitutionalism and Social Justice.
 
The Grand Auditorium of the university was packed to capacity with over six hundred attendees : prominent high court jurists, constitutional scholars from Islamabad and Lahore, international human rights delegates, and scores of conservative provincial professors.
 
My name was listed on the afternoon plenary panel : *'Customary Law versus Statutory Rights : The Case of South Punjab.'*
 
Sitting in the front row, wearing her trademark designer sunglasses and flanked by her influential political relatives, was Umme Rubab. She had spent the preceding weeks spreading sneering remarks across the faculty cafeteria that my fieldwork was nothing more than an illiterate girl's fabricated propaganda.
 
When the moderator announced my name, a tense murmur rippled through the hall.
 
I stepped up to the grand wooden podium. I wore my simple, modest black abaya and my mother's white cotton chador, completely devoid of jewelry or cosmetic vanity.
 
Instead of reading from a dry theoretical paper, I switched on the multimedia projector.
 
Across the massive screen behind me flashed high-resolution digital scans of actual land mutation records from the Muzaffargarh District Archives, juxtaposed with the translated, signed affidavits of forty-two rural women who had been coerced at gunpoint into surrendering their Quranic inheritance rights.
 
"Ladies and gentlemen," I began, my voice clear, resonant, and steady, cutting through the hall's acoustics like a silver bell. "We are told by conservative theologians and feudal apologists that the Eastern family structure is sacred, that a woman's glory lies in her silent sacrifice, and that legal intervention into family inheritance will destroy our traditional social fabric."
 
I pointed to the scan of an inheritance mutation signed with the bloody thumbprint of a terrified young mother.
 
"Look upon this screen," I continued, looking directly into the audience. "This is not tradition; this is grand larceny under the color of custom! This is the systematic, unconstitutional economic disenfranchisement of half our nation's citizens, perpetrated by brothers who use the sacred name of honor to rob their own sisters of bread!"
 
A collective, shocked intake of breath swept through the six hundred listeners.

During the question-and-answer period, a prominent conservative legal scholar from a traditional seminary stood up, adjusting his embroidered turban, his face flushed with indignation.
 
"Miss Rija," he challenged aggressively, his voice amplified by the roving microphone. "Your presentation carries the unmistakable, dangerous stench of Western secular feminism. In our glorious Islamic culture, a woman is protected, honored, and maintained by her father, husband, and brothers. Why do you wish to poison our daughters with greed for worldly land, fracturing the harmony of the Muslim household?"
 
The room fell into an electric, breathless silence. All eyes turned toward the podium, waiting to see if the young researcher would falter before the senior theologian.
 
I smiled faintly, leaned toward the microphone, and responded with devastating scriptural mastery.
 
"Respected Maulana Sahib," I began, my voice echoing with majestic, unshakeable calm. "It is profoundly telling that whenever a woman demands her clear, non-negotiable economic rights granted explicitly by Almighty Allah in the Holy Quran, she is immediately accused of Western secularism!"
 
The audience leaned forward.
 
"In Surah An-Nisa, verse seven," I continued, reciting the Arabic text flawlessly from memory : *'For men is a share of what the parents and close relatives leave, and for women is a share of what the parents and close relatives leave, be it little or much : an obligatory share ordained by Allah!'*
 
"Allah did not make female inheritance an optional charity contingent upon the generosity of brothers!" I proclaimed, my voice rising with righteous authority. "He decreed it as an absolute, divine obligation! Was the Mother of the Believers, Hazrat Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her, a greedy secularist when she operated the largest merchant caravan in Arabia? Was Hazrat Aisha a Western agent when she led armies, taught Islamic jurisprudence, and corrected the legal judgments of senior companions?
 
"No, Maulana Sahib! The Western secularist is not the daughter who demands her Quranic share; the true enemy of Islam is the feudal hypocrite who wraps his pagan tribal greed in religious slogans to steal what his Creator has bestowed upon his sister!"
 
For two seconds, the silence in that massive auditorium was absolute.
 
Then, like a sudden rolling wave of thunder, the former Chief Justice of the Lahore High Court, seated in the center of the front row, rose to his feet and began clapping vigorously!
 
Within seconds, the entire auditorium : professors, students, foreign delegates, and jurists : stood up in a roaring standing ovation that rattled the glass chandeliers!
 
Tears spilled down my cheeks as I stood before that cheering assembly. In the front row, Umme Rubab was gathering her handbag in furious, humiliated silence, hastily fleeing through the side exit to escape the spectacle of my absolute triumph.
 
Dr. Masood stood near the stage wings, his eyes shining with profound paternal pride, nodding slowly.
 
Season Three had reached its glorious, incandescent zenith.
 
I had broken the silence. I had dragged the truth from the darkness of the archives into the brilliant light of day. And no power on earth could ever force that truth back into the shadows.
