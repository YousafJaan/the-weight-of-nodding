# Episode 38: In the Senate Hall
### The Grand Senate Chamber, The External Examiner, and The Public Defense

**Season 4 : The Crucible of Intellect** | Words: 681

> **Synopsis:** Inside the historic Senate Hall of BZU, before an audience of over four hundred scholars, judges, and students, Rija defends her thesis before external examiner Dr. Humayun Khalid, Vice Chancellor of QAU Islamabad.

---

The Senate Hall of Bahauddin Zakariya University was the grandest, most imposing academic chamber in Southern Punjab. Constructed during the British Raj with high cathedral ceilings, dark teak wood paneling, massive stained-glass windows, and oil portraits of historic chancellors lining the walls, it was reserved exclusively for presidential convocations and highest-tier doctoral and master's public defenses.
 
On that crisp December morning, the hall was filled to standing capacity with over four hundred people : faculty members from across the university, prominent practicing advocates from the High Court Bar Association, journalists, human rights researchers, and hundreds of undergraduate students.
 
Seated in the reserved family gallery on the left wing were Mother and Father. Father wore his spotless white cotton kurta, his best black waistcoat, and his crisp white ancestral turban, sitting on the very edge of the wooden bench, his hands gripping his knees so tightly his knuckles were white.
 
At precisely ten o'clock, the examination board entered the hall through the ceremonial double doors.
 
Leading the panel was the Chief External Examiner : Professor Dr. Humayun Khalid, Vice Chancellor of Quaid-i-Azam University Islamabad, a former Chief Justice of the Federal Shariat Court, and internationally renowned authority on Islamic jurisprudence and constitutional rights.
 
Beside him sat Dr. Masood, beaming with quiet confidence, while Dr. Arshad sat at the far end, looking nervous and grim.
 
"The public viva voce defense of candidate Miss Rija Sajjad for the degree of Master of Philosophy in Political Science is now formally in session," the university registrar announced into the microphone.
 
I walked up the carpeted steps to the grand carved podium in the center of the stage.
 
I wore my simple black abaya and my mother's white cotton chador. In front of me rested my 350-page dissertation and fifty selected visual exhibits.
 
For the next forty-five minutes, I delivered a masterclass in empirical legal scholarship.
 
Without notes, speaking with effortless, resonant authority, I guided the assembly through the statistical architecture of disinheritance across Southern Punjab : dissecting the psychological mechanisms of coerced *Haq Bakhshwana*, mapping the geographic distribution of fraudulent land mutations, and demonstrating how the customary practices of the agrarian elite directly violated both statutory Pakistani penal law and the explicit injunctions of the Holy Quran.

When my presentation concluded, the formal examination by Dr. Humayun Khalid commenced.
 
Dr. Khalid was legendary across the country for his razor-sharp, merciless intellectual interrogations. For nearly an hour, he grilled me with relentless forensic intensity : challenging my statistical sample sizes, questioning the evidentiary chain of custody for sixty-year-old revenue ledgers, and testing my comprehension of complex inheritance shares under Hanafi and Ja'fari jurisprudence.
 
Every single question he hurled at me was met with an instant, precise, devastating response.
 
When he challenged my interpretation of Section 498-A of the Penal Code, I recited the exact statutory text and cited three landmark Supreme Court appellate rulings from memory, complete with volume numbers and page citations.
 
When he questioned the authenticity of the rural women's affidavits, I produced the registered video and audio transcripts, offering certified identification verification for every single witness.
 
The audience sat in absolute, breathless silence. Not a chair scraped; not a whisper sounded.
 
Finally, after a grueling ninety minutes of continuous cross-examination, Dr. Humayun Khalid paused.
 
He took off his gold-rimmed reading glasses, placed them slowly upon my open dissertation, and leaned back in his leather chair.
 
For ten long, agonizing seconds, he stared at me across the stage.
 
Then, a slow, magnificent smile broke across his dignified, weathered face.
 
He picked up his fountain pen, signed the official evaluation ledger with a flourish, and tapped the microphone.
 
"Miss Rija," Dr. Khalid said, his resonant baritone filling every corner of the vast Senate Hall. "In my thirty-five years of academic and judicial life in this country and abroad, I have presided over more than three hundred doctoral and master's defenses. But today, I must state without hesitation that I have never witnessed a defense of such breathtaking empirical rigor, flawless constitutional precision, and moral brilliance."
 
A collective gasp of awe swept through the four hundred listeners.
