# Episode 45: Live Broadcast in the Village
### The Village Tea Stall, The Stunned Patriarchs, and The Collapse of Malice

**Season 5 : The Turban and the Dawn** | Words: 582

> **Synopsis:** Back in the ancestral village, the entire community gathers around the sole television set at the crossroads, watching Rija's live presidential address in stunned silence as Aunt Shabana and Taya's authority collapses.

---

While the crystal chandeliers of the Convention Centre blazed in Islamabad, three hundred miles to the south, inside our ancestral village, an unprecedented social revolution was unfolding around a dusty fourteen-inch television screen.
 
At eleven in the morning, the village grocer, Chacha Kareem, had placed his black-and-white television set upon a wooden table outside his tea stall at the central crossroads, tuning the antenna to Pakistan Television's live state broadcast.
 
Word had leaked that Sajjad's daughter was appearing before the President.
 
Within twenty minutes, an immense crowd of over two hundred village men : farmers, laborers, shopkeepers, and youth : had gathered in a dense, breathless circle around the tea stall, standing upon tractor-trolleys and sitting upon rope beds.
 
Seated on a carved wooden chair in the front row, holding his silver hookah with performative indifference, was Taya Hakim Khan, flanked by his sons Farooq and Arslan.
 
Taya had arrived intending to mock the proceedings, loudly declaring to the grocer : "They give these paper certificates to thousands of useless students. It means nothing. Sajjad has wasted his money on a useless city trip."
 
Then, the television screen flickered, and the national broadcaster announced : *'We now take you live to the Presidential Hall for the conferment of the National Gold Medal upon Miss Rija Sajjad.'*
 
The crowd fell into an absolute, petrified silence.

Across that small television screen, broadcast in crisp high-definition, appeared the grand stage of the Presidency.
 
And there, walking across the crimson carpet beneath the blazing lights, was Rija.
 
She did not look like an impoverished village girl; she looked like a sovereign queen, walking with the majestic grace of unshakeable nobility, wearing her mother's white chador.
 
The crowd gasped audibly as the President of Pakistan stepped forward, bent down, and placed the heavy golden medallion around her neck, embracing her like his own daughter.
 
When Rija walked up to the presidential lectern and her resonant, powerful voice began booming through the tea stall's small speaker, the effect upon the village men was electrifying.
 
As she spoke of the disinherited daughters, of the forced marriages, of the shotgun held to an innocent sister's head, several elderly farmers in the crowd lowered their heads, tears glistening in their weathered beards.
 
"By God," whispered the village blacksmith, his voice shaking with awe. "Sajjad's daughter is not an ordinary woman. She is a saint of knowledge. Look at her : the rulers of the country are standing for her!"
 
Taya Hakim Khan's face had turned the color of cold ash. His hand shook so violently that the silver hookah pipe fell from his grasp, clattering onto the dirt.
 
Arslan sat with his head buried between his knees, unable to meet the mocking, pitying glances of the surrounding villagers. The delinquent who had threatened to beat Rija into submission was now permanently branded across the district as a pathetic, rejected brute.
 
Aunt Shabana, who had been watching from behind the brick parapet of her roof across the street, quietly turned and fled inside her house, locking her doors in total, humiliating defeat.
 
The feudal fortress that had held our family captive for three generations had crumbled into dust in forty minutes.
 
The village schoolmaster stood up in the center of the crossroads, raised his walking stick to the sky, and shouted with all his might : **"NARAA-E-TAKBEER! ALLAHU AKBAR! LONG LIVE RIJA SAJJAD!"**
 
And two hundred village men roared in unison, their voices echoing across the wheat fields toward our ancestral home.
