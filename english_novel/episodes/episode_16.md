# Episode 16: What Will People Say
### The Social Boycott, The Village Whispers, and The Chains of Hypocrisy

**Season 2 : The Courtyard of Chains** | Words: 717

> **Synopsis:** The terrifying aftermath of the broken match : the entire village ostracizes Father's household, locking the family into a suffocating quarantine of collective shame.

---

That Eid morning, our ancestral courtyard was not a house of celebration; it was a morgue where a living daughter was being buried beneath the suffocating dirt of social hypocrisy.
 
Traditionally, following the congregational Eid prayers, the men of the village moved in festive processions from house to house, embracing neighbors, exchanging sweet rice, and congratulating elders. But on that morning, the heavy iron knocker upon our wooden gateway remained entirely silent.
 
Through the cracks of the weathered wooden door, I watched the men in their crisp, starched white kurtas walk down our alley. When they approached our threshold, their conversations ceased abruptly; they cast dark, sideways glances toward our compound, whispered behind cupped hands, and quickened their pace as if passing a lazaretto contaminated with the plague.
 
Father did not attend the Eid prayer at the grand mosque. He had wrapped an old brown woolen shawl tightly over his head and face, lying motionless upon a bare rope charpai in the corner of the veranda, his back turned to the world. When Mother gently offered him a small porcelain bowl of warm vermicelli, he struck her hand away with a violent, trembling swipe, sending the bowl crashing onto the bricks.
 
"Take this filth away from my sight!" he groaned, his voice muffled by the blanket. "As long as that blackened girl draws breath beneath this roof, even the water of this house tastes of poison. The grocer refused to look me in the eye this morning; the imam turned his head away when I stepped into the lane. I am a dead man walking among the living."
 
Those four cursed words : *'Log Kya Kahenge'* (What will people say?) : are the invisible, bloodstained executioner's rope that has throttled millions of innocent daughters across the subcontinent.
 
These 'people' never step forward to pay a single school fee when a poor father's hands are calloused with labor; these 'people' never bring a loaf of bread when the crop fails and a family starves in silence; yet, let a malicious rumor be cast upon a daughter's virtue, and these very 'people' immediately gather with sharpened stones to enact their ritual of moral slaughter.

In the late afternoon, Aunt Shabana made her grand, performative entrance, accompanied by her two eldest daughters. She wore a look of theatrical, exaggerated mourning, draping her embroidered shawl over her face as she waddled across the courtyard.
 
"Bhabhi! Oh, my poor, ruined sister-in-law!" she wailed loudly, ensuring her lamentation was audible across the adjoining mud walls. "The entire district is talking! At the flour mill, at the tailor's shop, even at the veterinary clinic : every man is discussing Rija's Multan scandals! They are saying that Sajjad's daughter was rejected on the night of Eid because her sins were laid bare before the respectable families of Faisalabad!"
 
Mother stood up from the hearth, her voice trembling with fragile defense : "Shabana, my Rija is pure. She has sworn upon the Holy Book that these are filthy slanders."
 
"Oaths upon the Book?" Aunt Shabana sneered, tossing her shawl back with calculated malice. "Even thieves and murderers swear on the Book in the local magistrates' courts! The issue is not what happened in secret; the issue is that her market value has been reduced to ash! No respectable, honorable family will ever accept a girl whose name has been dragged through university dormitories. If you have an ounce of wisdom left, convince Sajjad to fall at Taya Hakim Khan's feet. Beg Arslan to take her off your hands. He is your own nephew; he will keep her locked behind his walls and throw a veil over this public catastrophe!"
 
Every word that emerged from her mouth was dipped in generational, woman-on-woman venom : that peculiar, terrifying sadism of patriarchal matriarchs who find supreme pleasure in seeing another woman's wings clipped.
 
I stood behind the half-opened door of the pantry, watching this grotesque circus of false piety. For the first time, the paralyzing terror of 'What will people say' snapped completely inside my consciousness. I looked at Aunt Shabana, and I felt only an immense, cold pity. I realized that the opinions of these village hypocrites were not a divine judgment; they were merely the barking of frightened dogs trapped in a cage of their own ignorance.
