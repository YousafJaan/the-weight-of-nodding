# Episode 15: The Poison of Chand Raat
### The Moon of Eid, Black Henna, and The Midnight Telephone Call

**Season 2 : The Courtyard of Chains** | Words: 1,103

> **Synopsis:** On the celebratory eve of Eid al-Fitr, a prospective outside match engineered by Father collapses when the groom's family demands an exorbitant car dowry and repeats Umme Rubab's hostel slander over a devastating late-night call.

---

It was the sacred eve of Eid al-Fitr : *Chand Raat* : the night when the thin, silver crescent moon appears in the dusk sky, ushering in joy, forgiveness, and renewal across the Muslim world. In the village, the air was alive with the laughter of children setting off firecrackers, the sweet fragrance of roasting vermicelli, and young girls singing wedding folk songs as they adorned their hands with fragrant green henna paste.
 
Inside our home, a fragile, desperate hope had briefly flickered.
 
In a quiet attempt to escape the violent trap of Taya Hakim Khan's forced marriage, Father had secretly reached out to a distant relative in the industrial city of Faisalabad. Their eldest son was an officer in a private commercial bank, and his family had visited our home two months prior, expressing keen admiration for my academic credentials. Father had hoped that this respectable match would provide an honorable exit, allowing him to defy Taya without losing face.
 
Mother had brought out an old porcelain bowl containing fresh henna, carefully pressing a delicate green pattern onto my right palm.
 
"Rija, my sweet child," Mother whispered, her eyes shining with hopeful tears in the lantern light. "May tomorrow's Eid dawn bring deliverance to our home. The boy's mother promised your father that she would call tonight after the moon sighting to finalize the date of the formal engagement. Insha'Allah, you will leave this valley of wolves with honor."
 
At twenty minutes past eleven, while the village mosques announced the sighting of the Shawwal moon over the loudspeakers, the antique telephone on the small wooden table in the central veranda began to ring.
 
Father rushed forward, his hands trembling as he lifted the heavy plastic receiver. Because the earpiece was worn and the line carried a loud echo, the harsh, shrill voice on the other end was clearly audible across the quiet courtyard.
 
It was the bank officer's mother, Mrs. Tariq. But the polite, solicitous tone she had displayed during her tea visit was entirely gone, replaced by an icy, razor-sharp hostility.
 
"Listen to me, Sajjad Sahib," the woman began without the courtesy of an Eid greeting. "We were prepared to overlook the fact that your family belongs to a backward rural background. But yesterday, my nephew, who works at the Multan Secretariat, conducted some discreet inquiries at Bahauddin Zakariya University regarding your daughter."
 
Father's face instantly drained of color, his posture sagging against the veranda post. "Sister... what could anyone say? My daughter is pure as morning dew..."
 
"Save your speeches, Sajjad Sahib!" the woman interrupted with a mocking laugh. "We have detailed reports! Her name was involved in an ugly public scandal regarding an international research grant! Girls in her own hostel were openly discussing her late-night private visits to male professors' chambers! The entire campus knows that she is an independent, loose-tempered female who cannot be managed by any decent family!"
 
Father gripped the wooden post, beads of cold sweat breaking across his forehead. "This is slander, Sister! Wicked lies fabricated by jealous rivals!"
 
"Lies or truth, our family has a sterling reputation to preserve," Mrs. Tariq countered with brutal, transactional coldness. "If you expect my son to marry a girl whose chastity has been dragged through university corridors, the compensation must match the risk. Our demand is firm : you will provide a brand-new Honda City sedan, five tolas of pure gold, and five hundred thousand rupees in cash deposited in my son's account before the nikah. If this is acceptable, we will bring the bridal procession next week. If not, keep your tainted scholar daughter locked in your courtyard forever!"
 
The line went dead with a violent, final click.

The plastic receiver slipped from Father's numb fingers, dangling by its coiled black cord, emitting a dull, buzzing dial tone like the humming of a funeral dirge.
 
Father stood completely motionless for nearly thirty seconds. His eyes, normally brown and tired, were now bloodshot with a terrifying, apocalyptic mixture of absolute humiliation, wounded patriarchal vanity, and murderous fury.
 
He turned slowly toward where Mother and I sat beneath the lantern.
 
"Rija!" His roar tore through the quiet night like an artillery shell.
 
Mother screamed, jumping up to shield me, but Father shoved her aside with violent, frantic strength. He lunged across the rug, grabbed my graduation certificate from the wooden shelf, and with three savage, trembling tears, ripped the heavy parchment into shredded ribbons, scattering the pieces across the dirt floor.
 
"Is this the reward you brought to my white beard?" he screamed, his voice cracking into an agonizing, broken sob of pure hatred. "I sent you to the city to study, and you turned my family name into a filthy joke across two provinces! Outside strangers call my home on the night of Eid to tell me that my daughter is a bazaar woman! They demand a ransom to hide your shame!"
 
"Father! Look at me!" I cried out, dropping to my knees and desperately clutching the hem of his trousers. "I swear by the Lord of the heavens and the earth, every word that woman spoke was a vile, fabricated poison planted by Umme Rubab because I defeated her on merit! I have never allowed a stranger's hand to touch my garment! Father, for the love of Allah, believe your own child!"
 
"Do not touch me with your defiled hands!" he bellowed, kicking his leg violently backward, tearing his hem from my grasp. "A daughter's purity is not proven by her own tongue! If the world says you are blackened, you are blackened! I wish you had choked on your mother's milk and died on the day you were born! Then I would not have lived to see this night of supreme disgrace!"
 
He stormed into his inner chamber and slammed the heavy wooden door shut, bolting it from within.
 
Mother collapsed upon the courtyard floor, beating her chest in silent, suffocating hysteria.
 
I sat alone in the dust beneath the flickering amber lantern. Scattered around my knees were the torn, shredded fragments of my university degree, trampled into the chicken droppings and mud.
 
I looked down at my right palm. The wet green henna paste was now smeared with the salty runoff of my own tears, turning into an ugly, blackened stain. In that terrifying midnight hour, as the celebratory Eid cannons echoed from the distant village square, I understood with chilling finality that in this society, a daughter's innocence is an empty leaf, and that her father would rather see her corpse in a shroud than face the whispered rumors of a hypocritical world.
