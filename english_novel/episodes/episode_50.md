# Episode 50: The Eternal Dawn of Rija
### The Open Gateway, The Rising Sun, and The Torch of Hope

**Season 5 : The Turban and the Dawn** | Words: 595

> **Synopsis:** The grand finale of the epic novel : standing on the threshold of her ancestral courtyard at sunrise, Rija reflects upon her transformation from an oppressed rural daughter into an empowered national scholar, dedicating her life to every daughter of Pakistan. The eternal meaning of Rija (Hope).

---

The morning following the great reconciliation broke over Southern Punjab with a sublime, breathtaking brilliance that seemed to set the very heavens on fire.
 
The winter fog had rolled back toward the river, leaving the sky a vast, immaculate expanse of crystalline turquoise.
 
I woke before the dawn call to prayer, performed my ablutions with the cool groundwater of our courtyard hand-pump, and performed my Fajr prayers in the central veranda.
 
Then, wrapped in my mother's white chador, carrying my leather research satchel, I walked across the courtyard and stood upon the open wooden threshold of our family gateway.
 
Four years prior, on a scorching September noon, I had crossed this very threshold in terror, clutching my mother's prayer slip, bearing the suffocating burden of my father's warning that female honor was a fragile glass cup waiting to shatter in the mud.
 
Today, I stood upon that same threshold, an MPhil graduate with highest honors, a university medalist, an internationally published legal scholar, and the recipient of the National Gold Medal from the President of Pakistan.
 
I looked across the sleeping village.
 
In the pale morning light, the mud-brick walls, the vast green wheat fields stretching toward the canal banks, and the tall date palms swaying in the morning breeze looked renewed, cleansed of their ancient shadows.
 
From the chimney of the blacksmith's cottage, a thin plume of blue woodsmoke rose into the clear air. In that cottage lived one of the thirty-five children from my Jute Mat Academy : a little girl named Fatima who had won first prize in English composition last week, who now dreamed of becoming a doctor.
 
The seeds planted in the mud were already growing into forests of light.

The sun crested the eastern horizon, sending shafts of incandescent gold leaping across the wheat fields, striking the wooden gate, enveloping my figure in a warm, radiant mantle of light.
 
I touched the gold medal resting beneath my coat, feeling its cool, reassuring weight against my heart.
 
This autobiography is not merely the story of Rija Sajjad.
 
It is the living, breathing testament of every daughter of this ancient land who has ever sat in the darkness of a rural courtyard, listening to the men decide her fate, feeling the terrifying walls of tradition closing in upon her soul.
 
To every young woman reading these words : When your family tells you that your dreams are a curse upon their honor; 
When the world threatens you with the poison of 'What will people say'; 
When the cowards corner you with weapons and demand the surrender of your intellect; 
When every earthly door is bolted against your name and you sit in the dark upon a sack of grain : **DO NOT BOW YOUR HEAD.** 
**DO NOT SURRENDER YOUR SOUL.**
 
You are not a fragile glass cup waiting to be broken. 
You are the steel from which the swords of justice are forged! 
You are the breath of life in this dying world!
 
Arm your mind with knowledge. Arm your soul with faith in the Living God. Stand tall upon the threshold of your convictions, and the mountains of oppression will crumble before your feet.
 
In the Arabic language, my name : **RIJA** (*رجاء*) : means **HOPE**.
 
Not a fragile, passive hope that sits in the corner weeping for rescue, but an incandescent, militant hope that marches into the furnace of adversity and demands the dawn!
 
The sun of Rija has risen over the plains of Pakistan.
 
And its light shall never, ever be extinguished.
 
**THE END OF THE EPIC SAGA**
