# Episode 43: The Gold Medal Around the Neck
### The Presidential Citation, The 24-Karat Gold, and The Climax of Justice

**Season 5 : The Turban and the Dawn** | Words: 550

> **Synopsis:** On the grand stage of the National Convention Centre, before television cameras and the national leadership, the President of Pakistan places the National Research Gold Medal around Rija's neck.

---

The Master of Ceremonies, an eminent broadcaster with a commanding, resonant voice, stood at the central lectern beneath the national seal.
 
"Ladies and gentlemen, we now proceed to the conferment of the highest civil academic distinction of this nation : The National Youth Research Excellence Award in Constitutional Law and Social Jurisprudence."
 
The hall grew quiet.
 
"The recipient this year has achieved an unprecedented standard of scholarly courage, conducting dangerous primary empirical research to expose the systemic deprivation of women's inheritance in Southern Punjab. Her master's dissertation has been adopted by the Law Commission as a foundation for legislative reform."
 
The broadcaster paused, his voice ringing with immense gravity : **"The National Gold Medal is awarded to : MISS RIJA SAJJAD, Bahauddin Zakariya University Multan!"**
 
The entire convention center erupted into polite, dignified applause as the spotlight shifted directly onto my seat.
 
I rose from my chair, kissed my mother's cheek, adjusted my white chador, and walked up the crimson carpeted steps toward the grand presidential dais.
 
The President of Pakistan : a distinguished elder statesman in a charcoal sherwani : stepped forward from the center of the stage, flanked by the Military Secretary and the Federal Minister for Education.
 
As I stood before the Head of State, looking respectfully into his eyes, the President smiled with profound, fatherly warmth.
 
The Military Secretary opened an open velvet presentation case lined with crimson satin.
 
Inside rested the National Gold Medal : a massive, heavy medallion of pure twenty-four-karat gold, suspended from a wide green and white silk ribbon, bearing the embossed seal of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan and the inscription : *'FOR COURAGEOUS SCHOLARSHIP AND CONSTITUTIONAL JUSTICE : 2014'*

The President lifted the heavy golden medallion, stepped forward, and gently placed the green ribbon around my neck, settling the gold against my chest.
 
He handed me the official citation scroll encased in carved silver filigree, took both of my hands into his, and spoke into the microphone : "Daughter Rija, on behalf of the two hundred million citizens of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan, I salute your courage. You have honored your university, you have elevated your province, and you have become a beacon of light for every daughter of this soil. This nation is forever in your debt."
 
The entire auditorium : cabinet ministers, Supreme Court justices, foreign ambassadors, and generals : stood up in a roaring standing ovation!
 
Dozens of camera flashes illuminated the stage in a dazzling, blinding storm of white light.
 
I looked down at the gold medal resting against my chest. Its surface was cold and heavy against my abaya, reflecting the brilliance of the crystal chandeliers.
 
In that sublime second, I remembered the crumpled five-rupee notes from the village mothers; I remembered the three hundred rupees Sara had pooled for our camping gas stove; I remembered the tears that had blurred my bank challan.
 
This gold was not mine. It belonged to every woman who had ever been silenced in the dust.
 
The President stepped aside and gestured toward the main presidential lectern.
 
"Speak to your nation, Daughter," the President said. "The country is listening."
 
I walked up to the microphone, looked out across the sea of faces, and prepared to deliver the message I had carried in my soul for four long years.
