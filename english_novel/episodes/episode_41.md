# Episode 41: The Letter from the Presidency
### The Green Courier Envelope, The Presidential Seal, and The Stunned Village

**Season 5 : The Turban and the Dawn** | Words: 524

> **Synopsis:** An official government dispatch from Aiwan-e-Sadr arrives in the village, notifying Rija of her selection for the prestigious National Youth Research Excellence Award in Islamabad.

---

Three weeks after my master's defense, on a crisp January morning when the winter sun was just burning off the morning frost, an official green postal van with government insignia drove directly into our village, stopping outside the post office.
 
The assistant postmaster, breathless and visibly agitated, sprinted down our alley on foot, clutching a heavy, stiff envelope of handmade parchment.
 
"Sajjad Sahib! Sajjad Sahib!" the postmaster shouted, banging on our wooden gateway. "A high-priority diplomatic dispatch has arrived for your household! It carries the gold seal of the President of Pakistan!"
 
Father hurried to the door, wiping his hands on his apron, his eyes wide with alarm. In our rural culture, an official government letter usually signaled a police summons, a land dispute, or a court order.
 
I stepped out into the courtyard as Father took the envelope.
 
Across the top of the parchment, printed in heavy raised gold leaf, was the official crest of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan : the crescent, the star, and the motto *Iman, Ittehad, Nazm* : alongside the embossed header : **OFFICE OF THE MILITARY SECRETARY TO THE PRESIDENT OF PAKISTAN** 
**AIWAN-E-SADR, ISLAMABAD.**
 
It was addressed in beautiful English calligraphy : *To : Miss Rija Sajjad, MPhil (Summa Cum Laude)* 
*Department of Political Science, Bahauddin Zakariya University Multan.*
 
With trembling fingers, I broke the scarlet wax seal and unfolded the heavy letter inside.

The letter was signed personally by the Federal Minister for Higher Education and countersigned by the Military Secretary to the President : *'Madam,* 
*I have the honor to inform you that upon the unanimous recommendation of the National Academic Evaluation Directorate and the Supreme Court Research Advisory Council, you have been selected to receive the prestigious : * 
**NATIONAL YOUTH RESEARCH EXCELLENCE AWARD & GOLD MEDAL** 
*in the field of Constitutional Jurisprudence and Agrarian Human Rights for the year 2014.* 
 
*The award will be conferred in person by His Excellency, the President of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan, at the National Convention Centre, Islamabad, on Tuesday, February 10, 2015, at 11 : 00 hours.* 
 
*Official state transport, air travel tickets, and executive protocol accommodations at the State Guest House in Islamabad have been reserved for you and two accompanying family members.'*
 
Father stood completely paralyzed, his jaw slack, staring at the golden crest.
 
"The President?" he whispered, his voice trembling so violently he could barely pronounce the syllables. "The President of the country is summoning my daughter to Islamabad?"
 
Mother let out a breathless, joyful sob, falling onto her knees right there in the courtyard gravel, pressing her forehead against the earth in a prostration of absolute gratitude.
 
Within an hour, the news exploded across the village.
 
Neighbors who had spent two years averting their eyes came crowding around our gateway, peering over the mud walls with expressions of stunned, bewildered awe. The village schoolmaster arrived in his best coat to congratulate Father; the local shopkeepers insisted on sending boxes of sweetmeats; and for the first time in thirty years, our humble courtyard was illuminated with the radiant light of national honor.
 
The dark storm had passed. The capital of the nation was calling.
